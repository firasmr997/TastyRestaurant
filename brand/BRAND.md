# Tasty brand identity

**Direction: Crate Label.** Tasty presents every dish the way growers presented their best harvest: a framed lithographed label, flat condiment inks, and a die-cut sticker for every state. It deliberately avoids the dark-moody-gold restaurant template.

All assets live in `frontend/public/brand/` and are generated by `brand/build-brand.mjs` (`cd brand && npm install && npm run build`). The lettering is outlined from the Big Shoulders typefaces (SIL Open Font License), so the logo renders identically everywhere, even before web fonts load.

## Logo system

| Asset | File | Use |
|---|---|---|

| Secondary logo (seal) | `tasty-seal.svg` | Round stamp for social avatars, stickers, packaging, the admin sign-in |
| Logo mark | `tasty-mark.svg` / `tasty-mark-sticker.svg` | The bitten sticker on its own; the `-sticker` variant has the white die-cut edge |
| Wordmarks | `tasty-wordmark-inline.svg`, `tasty-wordmark.svg` | Inline lettering for display moments; solid for small sizes |
| Favicon | `../favicon.svg`, `favicon-32.png`, `apple-touch-icon.png`, `icon-512.png` | Solid "T" variant, legible at 16 px |
| Social card | `../og-image.jpg` | 1200 × 630 Open Graph image |

**The mark.** A round produce sticker with a bite taken out of its edge and an inline **T** inside. The sticker ties the brand to the market-crate world; the bite says *taste* without a fork, knife or chef's hat. At small sizes the inline T switches to a solid T so the mark holds up as a favicon.

**Clear space** equals the height of the bite (about a fifth of the mark's diameter). **Minimum sizes:** mark 16 px (solid T), primary lockup 28 px tall. Don't recolour the mark outside the inks below, stretch it, add shadows to it or redraw the bite.

## Colour

Strategy: **full palette of condiment inks**. Pickle green owns large fields, tomato is the action ink, mustard marks stickers and highlights. Inks are flat: no gradients, no glows. Depth only comes from layers sitting on layers (stickers over photos, labels over fields).

## Components

- **Buttons.** Rounded 10 px. Primary is tomato with white condensed caps and an arrow that slides forward on hover; secondary is an outline in the current ink; on tomato fields the button inverts to label stock.
- **Ruled cells.** Navigation, menu categories, filters and language switch are a strip of cells divided by 1.5 px rules. The active cell floods tomato.
- **Label frame.** A heavy rule with a fine rule inside it (`.label-frame`), in mustard on dark page headers.
- **Cards.** A dish is a white label with an arched photo window, the name in condensed caps, two lines of description and a die-cut price sticker overlapping the window. Hover lifts the card and turns the sticker.
- **Stickers.** Round or oval flat ink with a white 3 px cut edge, slightly rotated: price (mustard), chef's pick (tomato), sold out (an ink band stamped across the photo), veggie / spicy chips.
- **Admin.** Same inks, quieter: pickle sidebar, tomato for the current section and primary actions, sticker-like status badges, Barlow throughout.

## Imagery

Food is always the loudest thing on the page. Photos sit in **arched windows** (semicircular top, small bottom radius) or full rectangles with 16 px corners. They are never tinted, filtered or overlaid with gradients: captions sit on solid label tabs. Placeholder photography comes from Unsplash; replace it with Tasty's own shoots through the admin (Food Images, Restaurant Gallery, dish photos) and nothing in the code changes.

## Motion

One authored moment per surface. **The label is stuck on:** on first visit the label appears, the sticker is slapped on with a little overshoot, the wordmark prints, then the label lifts away to reveal the hero, whose window opens and price sticker lands. Scroll reveals are short rises; parallax and 3D drift live on rear layers only, and the reading plane never moves. `prefers-reduced-motion` removes the splash animation, parallax, the pinned scroll, the 3D scene and large transforms.

## Voice

Confident, warm, specific and a little playful. Describe what happens in the kitchen ("smashed to order on a screaming-hot plancha"), never superlatives we can't prove. No invented awards, ratings or founding dates. Both languages are written, not machine-translated.
